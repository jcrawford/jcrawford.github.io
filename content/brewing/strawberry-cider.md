Pasteurized cider will work, but avoid anything containing potassium sorbate or sodium benzoate, since those will suppress fermentation. Hull the strawberries and crush them lightly — just enough to break the skins and release juice without turning them into complete pulp."
  - title: "Build the Must"
    description: "Pour the cider into your sanitized 1.5-gallon fermenter. Add the crushed strawberries directly, or place them in a sanitized mesh brew bag for easier removal later. If you want a stronger or slightly sweeter finished cider, stir in 1/4 to 1/2 cup of sugar or honey now.   - title: "Aerate and Pitch Yeast"
    description: "Take a gravity reading if possible — fresh cider usually starts around 1.050 and will finish near 1.000 for roughly 6-7% ABV. Add the yeast nutrient, then pitch your chosen yeast.   - title: "Primary Fermentation"

## Notes

This is a planned batch, not yet started. The idea is to blend the bright acidity of local apple cider with the aroma and color of fresh strawberries. Because cider ferments quickly and clears fast, this should be a shorter-turnaround project than the fruit meads.

**Yeast choice matters here.** EC-1118 will strip away most residual sugar and leave a dry, champagne-like cider. Nottingham ale yeast will ferment a little less aggressively and may leave a softer, more rounded finish that pairs well with strawberries.

**Strawberry timing:** Adding strawberries in primary gives the strongest color extraction but can blow off some delicate aromatics. A small secondary strawberry addition is worth considering if the color or flavor is too subtle after primary.
