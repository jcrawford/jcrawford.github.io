I bought a PLAATO Airlock because the pitch was irresistible: a smart airlock that counted fermentation bubbles, estimated gravity, and sent updates to my phone. It was one of those products that felt like the future of homebrewing. No samples, no hydrometer readings, no guesswork. Just sanitize the bubbler, connect it to the app, and watch the fermentation curve build itself. For around $100, I expected a few years of useful data. I got a few months before the whole platform collapsed.

## What It Was Supposed to Do

The idea was genuinely clever. Instead of opening the fermenter and pulling samples, the PLAATO Airlock measured CO₂ activity by counting bubbles through the airlock. That data fed into an app that tried to estimate gravity trends and fermentation completion. When it worked, it was the kind of tool that made you feel like you had a brewery dashboard in your pocket.

The hardware looked good too. A clean black unit with a minimalist Scandinavian design that fit into my fermenter setup without looking like a prototype. Setup was straightforward, the app paired quickly, and for the first few batches I actually enjoyed checking the graphs.

## Where It Fell Apart

The problems started with silence. Firmware updates slowed, then stopped. The app began throwing connection errors that would clear up after a restart, then errors that would not clear up at all. Then came the announcement: PLAATO was pivoting to commercial brewing applications and would no longer support the homebrew community. Reading that message felt like being told your car manufacturer had decided to focus on fleet vehicles and was turning off every consumer car on the road.

Shortly after the announcement, the app stopped working entirely. The cloud APIs that the device relied on were sunsetted, and without those endpoints the airlock could not authenticate, sync, or display data. There was no local mode, no open-source bridge, no way to repurpose the hardware. The device still powers on, still blinks, still connects to Wi-Fi, and still measures nothing useful because the backend it depends on is gone.

What makes it worse is how the company handled it. There was no meaningful migration path, no refund program, no commitment to keeping existing customers functional. The messaging was corporate and evasive, as if abandoning the people who funded the product was just a strategic pivot. It was not a technical failure. It was a deliberate choice to render paid hardware useless.

## Is It Worth Buying?

Absolutely not. Even if you find a used PLAATO Airlock for cheap, it is non-functional. There is no app worth installing, no cloud service feeding it data, and no developer community resurrecting it. It is a $100 plastic paperweight with a battery and a blinking light.

The PLAATO Airlock is the reason I now hesitate before buying any cloud-dependent brewing gadget. If a product needs someone else's server to stay alive, it is only as reliable as that company's business model. In this case, the business model changed, and every homebrew customer paid the price.

I would also caution any commercial brewers considering their current product line. If PLAATO was this quick to discard the homebrew community that funded their early growth, what is to say they will not pivot again and leave commercial operations without the tools and data they invested in? When a company treats its earliest adopters as disposable, it is a serious warning about long-term stability and commitment to any customer segment.

**Rating: 1 out of 5** — a good idea destroyed by a company that treated its customers like disposable beta testers.
